# Time — Project Concept (Initial Raw Model)

## Status
DEFINITION_TARGET / OPEN_QUESTION / NEW_FORMULA_TARGET / RELATION_TARGET

## User-proposed statements
1. Time is the measure of change.
2. Every thing is unique and different.
3. Each thing has its own timeline.
4. By “timeline”, the intended meaning is the object's unique temporal position/history: being born and eventually dying, while acknowledging that “timeline” is being used provisionally rather than as a standard definition.
5. To observe change, comparison requires another object/system or another state/part of the same object; change can be internal or external.
6. Everything is changing under a proposed view where change is connected to time and position.
7. Individual atoms may be treated as unique because of temporal and spatial position.
8. Unique atoms compose objects/compounds; vast numbers of individually differentiated things interact, form, diffuse, and affect one another.
9. Ignoring standard clock measurement, time may be object-/reference-dependent or relative to the system from which change is evaluated.
10. Change may always be unique.
11. Time may be perception- or perspective-dependent.

## Initial critique
- “Time is the measure of change” is promising but incomplete: measurement is not necessarily ontology. A clock measures a regular physical process; whether that process measures “time itself” is a deeper claim.
- “Every thing is unique and different” is too strong as written. Distinguish numerical identity, state, location, history, and intrinsic properties. Two entities may be qualitatively identical while remaining numerically distinct.
- “Each thing has its own timeline” is not yet a standard physical claim. It may be better formalized first as an object's worldline/history, state trajectory, or sequence of states/events.
- Birth/death gives temporal boundaries for some objects, not all entities. It cannot presently be universal unless the domain is restricted.
- “To observe change, comparison is required” is substantially stronger than “change requires comparison.” An observer may not need to compare explicitly for a physical state transition to occur. Observation and change must be separated.
- “Everything is changing” needs a domain and resolution. Some properties may remain invariant or unchanged over a specified interval; “everything changes” may depend on scale.
- “Uniqueness because of time and position” is not yet established. Two atoms of the same isotope can share the same intrinsic quantum numbers while differing in spacetime relation; whether position/time alone gives individuality depends on the identity criterion.
- “Time is different depending on objects” risks conflating coordinate/reference dependence with physical time itself. Relativity supports observer/frame dependence of temporal measurements, but this does not automatically mean every object possesses a distinct fundamental time.
- “Time is perception/perspective based” is presently ambiguous between psychological perception, reference-frame dependence, and epistemic measurement. These must be separated.
